How Pool Shield works

Pool Shield reads public Solana data and shows the warning signs a liquidity provider should understand before adding money to a pool. It is a learning tool, not a verdict. Version 2.3.

Where the data comes from

  • Solana RPC (a public blockchain node): token settings, and the largest holders.
  • Dexscreener: the trading pool, its price, liquidity, 24-hour volume, age and market cap.
  • RugCheck: how much of the pool's LP tokens are locked or burned.

Nothing is stored about the people who use the tool beyond anonymous visit counts and the token address that was scanned. The "Recent scans" list is kept only in your own browser.

How the score works

Points add up to a score out of 100. Up to 15 reads Lower risk, up to 45 reads Caution, and above that High risk. Regulated stablecoins (currently USDC, USDT and PYUSD) read Known issuer, because their issuers hold mint and freeze powers by design. The thresholds are judgement calls, not science. A grey row means a check could not be completed, and the page shows how many of the checks finished.

How wallets are told apart from pools

Normal wallets are addresses on a specific mathematical curve. Pools, vaults and bonding curves use program-controlled addresses that are deliberately off that curve, so they can be recognised without a hand-made list. Anything program-controlled is shown in its own row and not counted as a whale.

Top holders list

The ten largest holders are listed with a label (wallet, pool, program or burned) and a link to Solscan. Pools, programs and burned tokens are kept apart from wallets, but exchange and custodian wallets still appear as wallets.

How fresh the data is

Market data (price, liquidity, volume) is fetched live on every scan. With Live switched on, the page re-scans every 20 seconds while the tab is open, and the page shows when it was last updated. The on-chain safety checks (mint, freeze, holders) change slowly, so they are reused for up to 5 minutes. "Re-scan now" forces everything to be re-read.

What Pool Shield cannot see

  • Liquidity lock accuracy. The lock figure comes from RugCheck, not from Pool Shield's own reading of the chain. It can be unavailable, and older pools may hold LP in unlocked positions for good reasons.
  • Exchange and custodian wallets. They are counted like any other wallet, so a token with large exchange balances can look more concentrated than it is.
  • Bonding-curve pools. Dexscreener does not size them, so liquidity shows as unknown.
  • What is inside program-controlled accounts. A pool, a lock and a team multisig look the same here. They are shown but not scored, so a team-controlled vault could hide in that row.
  • Linked wallets. One person can split their tokens across many wallets. Pool Shield counts each wallet on its own.
  • Everything off-chain: who the team is, what the project does, and whether the website is honest.
